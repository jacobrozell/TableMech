Table size, or use the box maps

Alpha Strike is written for a 3'×4' to 4'×4' open surface, each player deploying along an opposite long edge. You don't need a mat — the two 18"×22" paper maps side by side give roughly 36"×22", plenty for four 'Mechs a side.

Playing on the printed hexes. Treat 1 hex = 2 inches and everything converts. A 'Mech with MV 12" walks 6 hexes. Short range becomes 3 hexes, medium reaches 12, long reaches 21. Use the map's own terrain instead of placing templates.

The turn, start to finish

Four phases, in this order, every turn, until one side meets the victory condition. The bar at the top tracks where you are and tells you who acts first.

Phase 1 — Initiative Both players roll

Each player rolls 2D6. Highest total wins the turn. Re-roll ties.

Winning initiative means acting last. Not a typo — everything in a turn happens simultaneously in fiction, so the player who read the battlefield better gets to react to what the enemy already committed to.

Roll to see who acts first.
Phase 2 — Movement Alternating

The initiative loser moves one 'Mech. Then the winner moves one. Alternate until every 'Mech has moved.

Moving commits a 'Mech to a movement mode for the turn — standstill, ground move, sprint or jump. That choice changes both how hard it is to hit and how well it shoots, so it's the real decision of the phase.

If one side has more 'Mechs left to move, it moves in proportion: with twice as many left, it moves two for every one of the opponent's.

Phase 3 — Combat Not alternating

The initiative loser declares and resolves attacks for all of their 'Mechs, one after another. Then the winner does the same.

Destroyed 'Mechs still shoot back. Damage is written on the card immediately, but its effects don't take hold until the End Phase. A 'Mech wiped out during the loser's attacks still fires in the winner's attacks that same turn.

Each 'Mech gets one attack: a weapon attack or a physical attack, never both.

Phase 4 — End Simultaneous
  1. Apply damage effects and remove destroyed 'Mechs from the table.
  2. Apply heat gained this turn, then cool down.
  3. Check victory conditions.

Then back to Phase 1.

Moving

MV is inches, not a pool of points. Use a flexible tape so curved paths measure honestly.

Standing still makes you accurate and easy to hit. Jumping makes you slippery and inaccurate. Sprinting is pure repositioning — no attack, and no grabbing objectives either.

  • Never more than 2" of elevation per 2" travelled. Anything steeper is impassable.
  • Minimum move: a mobile 'Mech can always move 2" regardless of terrain cost.
  • Jumping ignores terrain costs and takes the shortest path. It can jump into water but not out of it.
  • End facing any direction you like. Facing only matters for firing arcs and rear hits.
Stacking. You can move through friendly 'Mechs but not through enemies at the same level, and no two 'Mechs finish on the same spot. The whole base has to fit legally.

Shooting

Every attack is one 2D6 roll against a target number you build with the SATOR stack. Pick your two 'Mechs from the roster and the numbers fill themselves in.

Three checks first

  1. Line of sight. Crouch to the firing mini's eye level. If less than a third of the target is visible behind solid terrain, LOS is blocked. Woods only block once the line passes through 6" or more of them.
  2. Firing arc. The front arc runs forward from the model's facing. If more than half the target's base is outside it, no shot.
  3. Range. Measure base edge to base edge, then enter it below.
Nose to nose? 'Mechs in base-to-base contact can't shoot each other at all. Punch instead.

Damage, criticals and heat

Armor first, then structure. Any hit that bites into structure risks a critical.

Applying a hit

  1. Cross off one armor bubble per point of damage until armor runs out.
  2. Spill the rest into structure bubbles.
  3. If the attack marked any structure at all, the attacker rolls once on the critical table.
  4. When the last structure bubble is gone the 'Mech is destroyed, removed in the End Phase.

Heat

If the card shows an OV above zero you can announce before rolling that you're pushing it. Add up to OV points of damage at short or medium range and take the same number as heat in the End Phase. Only cards with OVL get that bonus at long range.

Heat levelWhat it costs next turn
1+1 to your target numbers, −2" MV
2+2 to target numbers, −4" MV, −1 TMM
3+3 to target numbers, −6" MV, −1 TMM
4Shutdown — no move, no attack, TMM drops to −4

Heat clears completely in any End Phase where the 'Mech made no weapon attack. Standing in water sheds 1 extra point. Jumping ignores the MV and TMM penalties.

Punching, kicking and worse

  • Standard physical — within 1", target in your arc. Damage equals Size.
  • Melee — only for cards showing MEL, reaches 2", damage is Size + 1.
  • Charge — declared in the Movement Phase, end in base contact. Damage is Size plus half your TMM; you take half your TMM back, +1 more if the target is Size 3 or larger.
  • Death From Above — jump onto them. Charge damage +1 and a free critical roll, but you take damage equal to your own Size.

A physical attack replaces the weapon attack for that turn.

When a 'Mech is finished

Under Forced Withdrawal a crippled 'Mech must head for its own board edge and leave. Crippled means: no armor left and down to half its starting structure, or both medium and long damage values reduced to 0, or immobilised by a critical. Withdrawing 'Mechs can still shoot anything that comes into range.

Your first two games

Written for the eight 'Mechs in your box. Game one teaches the turn in about forty minutes. Game two is the real thing.

Game 1 — Ridge Line 2 v 2 · ~40 min

Forces. One player takes the Shadow Hawk and Commando, the other the Wolverine and Locust. Everyone Skill 4. Two of these jump and two don't, which is exactly the contrast to feel first.

Table. Both paper maps side by side, or a 3'×3' surface. Four pieces of woods total, placed alternately, none within 4" of another. Nothing else.

Deploy. Alternate placing one 'Mech at a time within 6" of your own long edge.

Win. Destroy or cripple both enemy 'Mechs.

Skip for now. Heat, overheating, physical attacks, buildings, battlefield support. Just move, shoot, mark damage.

Game 2 — Full Lances 4 v 4 · ~90 min

Forces. Awesome, Thunderbolt, Shadow Hawk and Commando against BattleMaster, Catapult, Wolverine and Locust — one 'Mech from each weight class per side. Check point values on the Master Unit List and swap a light 'Mech across if the totals come out lopsided.

Table. Deploy along opposite long edges. Each player picks eight pieces of terrain from the punchboard. The initiative winner places one first, then alternate until all sixteen are down.

Win. Either last force standing, or first to 10 victory points scoring each destroyed or crippled enemy 'Mech's Size value.

Now turn on. Overheating, physical attacks and Forced Withdrawal.

Card conflict. This split puts the Awesome and the BattleMaster on opposite sides, and they're two sides of one card. Track one of them on the Roster tab so nobody is passing a card back and forth all evening.

What usually goes wrong on the first night

  • Forgetting the initiative winner acts last. Feels backwards for one game, then feels like an advantage.
  • Alternating attacks. Movement alternates one 'Mech at a time. Combat does not — the loser resolves everything, then the winner.
  • Removing casualties too early. They stay on the table and keep firing until the End Phase.
  • Measuring model to model. Range is base edge to base edge.
  • Missing rear shots. Check the attack direction on every hit — that free point adds up.
  • Forgetting the movement mode you chose. Drop a token or a die next to each 'Mech as it finishes moving.

Roster

Prefilled with the eight Alpha Strike cards from the box and a suggested four-a-side split. Type each card's numbers in once; after that, mark damage here and the Shoot tab pulls from these 'Mechs automatically.

How to use it. Type damage into the box and press Apply — armor fills first, then structure, and you'll be told when a critical roll is owed. Or click individual bubbles. Flip swaps a card to the variant printed on its back. Everything stays on this device.
